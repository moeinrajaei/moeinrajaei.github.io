My research integrates computational biology, evolutionary genetics, and large-scale sequencing analyses to investigate how mutation and selection shape genome evolution in natural populations and cancer. 

During my PhD, I investigated the evolution of mutational processes using *Caenorhabditis elegans* as a model system. By combining mutation accumulation experiments with whole-genome sequencing, I disentangled the contributions of mutation, drift, and selection and demonstrated how local sequence context shapes mutational spectra. I showed that mononucleotide repeat architecture drives distinct mutational signatures and explained discrepancies between mutations observed in laboratory lines and those in natural populations [Rajaei et al., 2021](/al-folio/publications/). This work established my interest in how genomic architecture and biochemical environment influence mutation rates, a theme that has continued into my cancer research.

As a postdoctoral fellow, I have focused on somatic evolution in cancer: examining how mutation, selection, and epistasis vary across disease stages. Working primarily in prostate cancer and extending into melanoma and thyroid cancer, I quantified the adaptive landscape from organogenesis through tumor initiation and metastatic progression and characterized the selective epistatic interactions that structure this evolutionary trajectory. These findings, currently under review [Rajaei et al., 2025](/al-folio/publications/), provide a comprehensive map of the evolving selective and epistatic forces that shape tumor progression across clinical stages.

I am interested in deepening our understanding of the processes that introduce mutations into the genome. I am exploring how genomic features influence mutation accumulation in contexts where selection is minimal, and I aim to extend these insights to early somatic evolution in humans. I hope to contribute fundamental insights into the central question in human cancer: How do the earliest mutational events shape the path toward tumorigenesis and progression?
